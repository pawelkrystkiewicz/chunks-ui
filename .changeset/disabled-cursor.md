Disabled form controls show the not-allowed cursor, and the dimmed look has one spelling

- A disabled `Input`, `Select.Trigger`, `Combobox.Control`, `Combobox.Input`, `Combobox.Trigger`, `Combobox.Clear`, `Combobox.ChipRemove`, `NumberField.Group`, `NumberField.Input`, `NumberField.Increment`, `NumberField.Decrement`, `Slider.Thumb`, `DatePicker` trigger, `ToggleGroup.Item`, `ClearButton` and a disabled `Calendar` day show the not-allowed cursor, as `Checkbox`, `Switch` and `Textarea` do. Before, `pointer-events: none` hid every cursor over them, so one disabled `Field.Root` showed a default cursor over its `Input` beside a not-allowed cursor over its `Textarea`. They ignore clicks as before: a disabled native input or button takes none, Base UI drops them on `Slider.Thumb` and `Combobox.ChipRemove`, and the `Calendar` day has no click handler. A click on a disabled `Calendar` day now focuses it and moves the grid's roving tab stop there (it is an `aria-disabled` button with `onFocus`), as keyboard navigation already does. A `Tooltip` around a disabled control now opens on hover, since the control receives the pointer. `Button`, `IconButton`, `Tabs`, the `Accordion` and `Collapsible` triggers, the `Menu` items, `Select.Item` and `Combobox.Item` keep `pointer-events: none` when disabled. To keep the old behaviour on a part that changed, pass `pointer-events-none` in `className`: `disabled:pointer-events-none` on the native inputs and buttons (`Select.Trigger`, `Combobox.Input`, `Combobox.Trigger`, `Combobox.Clear`, `NumberField.Input`, `NumberField.Increment`, `NumberField.Decrement`, `ToggleGroup.Item`, `ClearButton`), `data-disabled:pointer-events-none` on `Slider.Thumb` and `Combobox.ChipRemove` (which stays focusable when disabled), `has-[:disabled]:pointer-events-none` on `Combobox.Control`, `has-[input:disabled]:pointer-events-none` on `NumberField.Group`, `[&_button:disabled]:pointer-events-none` on `DatePicker` (its `className` goes on the wrapper) and `[&_td_[aria-disabled]]:pointer-events-none` on `Calendar` (a day has no class of its own). On `Input`, `disabled:pointer-events-none` works while the input has no adornment or `className` is a function; with an adornment a string `className` goes on the wrapper `<div>`, where `:disabled` never matches, so use `has-[input:disabled]:pointer-events-none` there.
- The hover styles of `ToggleGroup.Item`, `ClearButton`, `NumberField.Increment`, `NumberField.Decrement`, `Combobox.Clear`, `Combobox.ChipRemove` and the `Calendar` day stay off while disabled, because a disabled control now receives the pointer. The first six reset to the rest colour (`disabled:hover:`; `disabled:not-data-pressed:hover:` on `ToggleGroup.Item`, so a pressed item keeps its pressed colour; `data-disabled:hover:` on `Combobox.ChipRemove`, which stays focusable when disabled), and the `Calendar` day does not get its hover fill. `ClearButton` does not scale on a press while disabled. A `hover:` override in `className` still replaces the library's hover style and applies only while the control is enabled. The reset returns to the library's rest colour, so a rest-colour override in `className` (such as `text-primary` on `ToggleGroup.Item`, or a `bg-*` on `ClearButton`) needs a matching `disabled:hover:` class to hold while disabled.
- The dimmed look of `Radio.Root`, `Radio.Group`, `Checkbox.Root`, the `Menu` items, `Slider.Control`, `Slider.Thumb`, `Combobox.Item`, `NumberField.Increment` and `NumberField.Decrement` is spelled `data-disabled:`, as on `Label` and `Field.Label` (it was `data-[disabled]:`, which tailwind-merge treats as another class, so an override spelled `data-disabled:` stayed beside it and lost by stylesheet order). An override in `className` is now spelled `data-disabled:opacity-75`.
