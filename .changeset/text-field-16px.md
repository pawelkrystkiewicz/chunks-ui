Fixed: on iOS, Safari zoomed the page when `Input`, `Textarea`, `NumberField.Input` or `Combobox.Input` took focus, because their text was 14px and iOS zooms into a field with text under 16px. Their text is now 16px below the `md` breakpoint (768px by default, `max-md:text-base`) and on a touch screen at any width (`md:[@media(pointer:coarse)]:text-base` from `md` up), such as an iPad or a larger iPhone held sideways, and 14px otherwise, as before. `Select.Trigger` and the `DatePicker` trigger have the same size, so a form has one text size; `InputCopy`, which renders an `Input`, and your own elements through `inputVariants()` follow. Where the text is 16px, a `Textarea` row is 4px taller (24px), and a field that takes its width from its content is about 22px wider, because an input's default width follows its font size. These are an `Input` with adornments (`startAdornment`, `endAdornment`, `clearable` or `onClear`), every `InputCopy` and a `NumberField` in a flex row; a `Select.Trigger` sized by its value (`w-auto`) grows with its text. Give the field a width to keep the old one. With a mouse, a text-size class you pass, such as `text-lg`, still applies from `md` up (not on `DatePicker`, whose `className` goes on a wrapper). A `max-md:` class, such as `max-md:text-lg`, changes the size below `md`, and a `sm:` class, such as `sm:text-sm`, wins over the 16px from 640px to `md`, on a touch screen too. From `md` up, on a touch screen the 16px wins over these classes: to change the size there, pass a `md:[@media(pointer:coarse)]:` class, such as `md:[@media(pointer:coarse)]:text-lg`; a size under 16px brings the zoom back. On an `Input` with adornments and in `InputCopy`, a string `className` goes on the wrapper: use it for the width, but a text-size class there does not change the field. Pass a function, `className={() => "md:[@media(pointer:coarse)]:text-lg"}`, to reach the input. See Text Size in the `Input` docs.
