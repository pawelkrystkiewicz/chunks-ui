Added `Dialog.Body` (its props type, `DialogBodyProps`, is exported too), for dialogs with long content. Put it between the Title and the actions. The popup then becomes a flex column, at most the viewport height less 2rem: the Title, the Description and the actions stay in place, and only the Body scrolls. Without a Body, a tall popup scrolls as a whole (see the popup overflow entry) and, when its first tabbable element is a button in the footer, opens scrolled to it, with the Title out of view. Before, a tall dialog ran off the top and bottom of the viewport.

While its content overflows, the Body is a tab stop, so the keyboard can scroll it; it measures itself again when its box or its content changes, including a child that grows on its own, such as an image that loads or a list that fills in. It is a region that the Title names. When it overflows and nothing tabbable comes before it, it gets the focus when the dialog opens with a keyboard or a mouse, at the top of the content. A Body whose content fits is skipped, and the first action takes the focus. On touch, Base UI focuses the popup itself. While its content overflows it also has a `data-overflowing` attribute, from the same measurement as the tab stop, so a class can show that there is more to scroll: `<Dialog.Body className="data-overflowing:border-b">`. The attribute is absent while the content fits, and before the Body has measured (the server render).

The Body takes Base UI's `render`, so a form can be the Body: `<Dialog.Body render={<form id="new-task" onSubmit={…} />}>` keeps the scroll region, its name and its tab stop, and a `<Button type="submit" form="new-task">` in the actions outside the Body submits it. `Drawer.Body` takes it too. A `<div>` or a `<span>` with no `role`, as `render={<div />}` or returned by a function `render`, gets `role="region"`, the `<section>`'s role, so the Body's name is exposed on it. The role goes on only while the Body is named, by its Title or by an `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`, as a region must be. A `<form>` or another element with a role of its own, and a `role` you set on the Body or on the element, are left as they are.
